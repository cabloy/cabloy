## What the runtime proves, and does not prove

Normal cases cover pure admission precedence; exact and one-over JSON/UTF-8/depth/node/key/array/string/byte and header limits; bounded JSON/SSE output and native drain; real JSON/discovery without session echo; modern mirror/version/unknown-method/error codes, Base64 name and capability data; distinct rejected legacy initialize versus dropped notification; safe precommit and terminated postcommit failures; incomplete body/header receipt; held cleanup under disconnect/overlap with third-request 429; and 5-second cancellation followed by actual settlement.

Actual overlap sequence 280–307 in runtime 1 records two reserved/router-entered requests; disconnect/cancel observation while active remains 2; third-request 429 at sequence 292; only after the held cleanup is released do work/cleanup/disposal/executor/HTTP settle and reservations release. The deadline sequence 308–322 independently records 504, cancellation, native finish, adapter/work/cleanup/disposal/executor and final release. Successful HTTP delivery does not universally precede scope disposal; release waits for both, rather than falsely imposing one ordering on all paths.

Not qualified: production host-to-instance admission or full system middleware, OAuth AS/RS and protected-resource metadata, Passport/business authorization, tool catalog/eligibility/allowlist/fixed business dispatch, domain persistence/transactions/side effects, production quota/audit/log sinks or release. Exact writer/reader boundaries exercised through pure probes are not all real-wire parser cases; unsupported traffic remains finite fixture scope. The source-local watchdog exercise proves uncooperative case exit, not every possible factory/driver/startup hang. SDK close, socket close, timeout and runner app shutdown are not substituted for domain settlement.
